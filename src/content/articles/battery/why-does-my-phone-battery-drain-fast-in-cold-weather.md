Cold can change how much power a phone battery can deliver at that moment. The visible percentage, charging behavior, and any protective shutdown depend on the battery's condition, the phone's design, and its software. There is no single response shared by every phone.

## What battery science establishes

[The Edge et al. review](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2021/cp/d1cp00359c) separates capacity fade, power fade, and impedance increase, and explains that charging below freezing can promote lithium plating. It does not define a universal phone operating range, a fixed shutdown point, or a guaranteed warm-up time.

## A named Pixel example

[Google's safety guide for Pixel 7 and Pixel 7 Pro](https://support.google.com/product-documentation/answer/12164747?hl=en) says those models are designed to work best in ambient temperatures from 0 to 35 °C. If their internal temperature leaves the normal range, they may reduce performance or connectivity, stop charging, or power down. That is a Pixel 7 and Pixel 7 Pro example, not a range for every phone.

## What to do

Move the phone to a dry, room-temperature place and follow the instructions for the exact model. Before charging, wait until the phone is back inside its documented charging range and any temperature warning has cleared. No universal 15- or 30-minute waiting period is established.

Do not assume that a thick case will keep the battery safe or that every phone will shut down below zero. Case behavior, thermal design, charging limits, and protective software vary by device.

## Why an older battery may struggle more

A battery that already has capacity or power fade has less margin under load. Cold conditions may therefore expose an existing weakness, but the exact voltage cutoff and shutdown behavior are device-specific. If the problem repeats within the model's supported range, use the manufacturer's diagnostics or repair channel.
