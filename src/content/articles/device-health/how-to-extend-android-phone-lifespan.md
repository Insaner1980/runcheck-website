There is no fixed useful life shared by every Android phone. Battery condition, software support, storage pressure, physical damage, parts availability, and the cost of a model-specific repair all affect the decision.

## Battery ageing is cumulative, but not predictable from one rule

[The Edge et al. degradation review](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2021/cp/d1cp00359c) explains cell-level capacity fade, power fade, impedance increase, and the effects of temperature, state of charge, and load profile. These mechanisms interact and vary with chemistry, form factor, and prior use.

That evidence supports restrained habits: avoid repeated unnecessary heat and follow the charging guidance for the exact phone. It does not prove a universal 20–80% rule, a fixed phone lifetime, a particular case or charger, a repair procedure, or a guaranteed number of extra years.

## Keep supported software current

[Google's Android help](https://support.google.com/android/answer/7680439?hl=en) shows how to check the Android version, security update, and Google Play system update. The actual support period and update schedule are set by the manufacturer, model, region, and carrier. Check the model's current support page instead of relying on a brand-wide promise.

## Manage storage and physical condition

Back up important data and use the phone's own storage view to find large or unused files and apps. Follow the manufacturer's cleaning and repair instructions; ports, seals, batteries, displays, and replacement procedures differ by model. No universal repair price or accessory choice can determine whether keeping a phone is worthwhile.

## An EU regulatory floor, not a promise for every phone

[The European Commission's smartphone ecodesign page](https://energy-efficient-products.ec.europa.eu/product-list/smartphones-and-tablets_en) describes requirements for covered products placed on the EU market from 20 June 2025, including battery durability, spare-parts availability, and operating-system upgrade obligations. Those rules have a defined market and product scope; they do not retroactively give every existing phone the same support or repairability.

## Decide from the exact device

Check battery diagnostics and support status for the model, obtain a repair quote when needed, and compare the repaired device with the replacement you would actually buy. A repair can be sensible when the rest of the phone meets your needs, but the evidence does not guarantee a particular price, restored performance, or added lifetime.

The defensible goal is not to reach a universal number of years. It is to keep the specific phone safe, supported, backed up, and useful for as long as its condition and available support allow.
