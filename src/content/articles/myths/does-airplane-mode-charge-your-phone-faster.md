Airplane mode may shorten a charging session only when disabling radios reduces the phone's own power use. That is a conditional inference, not a guaranteed charging-speed feature, and no universal time or percentage applies.

## What the evidence establishes

[Google's Android battery guidance](https://support.google.com/android/answer/7664692?hl=en) recommends Airplane mode when mobile data is not needed as one way to reduce battery use. [USB Power Delivery](https://www.usb.org/usb-charger-pd) defines how compatible chargers and devices negotiate charging power. Neither source measures an Android phone charging faster in Airplane mode.

Together they support only this limited explanation: if the phone consumes less power while connected to the same charger, more of the incoming power may be available for charging. The effect can be negligible when the screen is off, signal is good, background activity is low, or charging is limited by the device, battery temperature, cable, or charger.

## When a difference is plausible

A difference is more plausible with weak mobile signal, active background traffic, or a low-power charger. Airplane mode also disconnects calls and mobile data. Wi-Fi or Bluetooth may remain enabled or be re-enabled depending on device settings, so the radio state must be checked rather than assumed.

Do not describe this as a fixed speed increase. Without a controlled test on the same phone, charger, cable, starting charge, temperature, and workload, any time saved is unknown.

## What matters more

Use a compatible charger and cable, keep the screen off, avoid heavy apps while charging, and keep the phone within its supported temperature range. If charging is unexpectedly slow, inspect the charger, cable, port, heat, and device charging settings before relying on Airplane mode.

Use Airplane mode only when you do not need to be reachable. It can reduce concurrent power use; it does not change the phone's supported charging protocol or maximum negotiated power.
