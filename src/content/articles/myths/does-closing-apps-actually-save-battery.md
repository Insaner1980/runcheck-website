For routine battery saving, do not keep swiping every app away. [Google's Android help says you do not usually need to close apps](https://support.google.com/android/answer/9079646?hl=en-GB) and recommends closing or force-stopping an app only when it is not responding.

## What the app switcher means

Android can keep recently used apps cached in memory so they reopen quickly. Cached does not automatically mean that an app is actively doing work. The [Android memory overview](https://developer.android.com/topic/performance/memory-management-overview) explains that the system reclaims app processes as memory is needed.

That memory behavior does not prove a fixed battery saving or penalty from closing an app. The defensible conclusion is narrower: routine clearing is unnecessary, and battery diagnosis should focus on apps that are actually misbehaving.

## When stopping one app makes sense

Close or force-stop an app when it has frozen or is clearly stuck. If an app is unexpectedly using location, audio, network activity, or a large share of battery, check its battery-use screen and its background permissions. Menu names vary by manufacturer and Android version.

Stopping one faulty app is troubleshooting. It is different from clearing every recent app as a daily battery ritual.

## What to do instead

Use the phone's battery-usage view to identify unusual activity, update or remove a faulty app, and restart the phone if a process remains stuck. Also check heat and poor mobile signal, because neither is fixed by emptying the recent-apps screen.

No universal battery percentage can be promised from closing or retaining recent apps. Measure the actual problem on your own device.
