Heat can accelerate lithium-ion battery ageing, but one phone-temperature number cannot be applied to every model. Cell chemistry, thermal design, battery condition, workload, charging control, and where the temperature is measured all matter.

## What the degradation review supports

[The Edge et al. review](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2021/cp/d1cp00359c) identifies temperature, state of charge, and load profile as external stress factors. It connects higher temperature with faster SEI growth and other degradation mechanisms that can produce capacity fade, power fade, and impedance increase. The mechanisms interact, and their importance varies with chemistry, form factor, and prior use.

That evidence supports a general cell-level statement: repeated or prolonged heat can contribute to ageing. It does not establish a universal 42 °C gaming or fast-charging threshold, generic under-35/over-40/over-45 phone rules, an exact phone lifetime, or a guaranteed outcome.

## A named manufacturer example

[Google's Pixel 7 and Pixel 7 Pro safety guide](https://support.google.com/product-documentation/answer/12164747?hl=en) says those models are designed to work best from 0 to 35 °C ambient temperature. Outside their normal internal range, they may reduce performance or connectivity, stop charging, or power down. This is a named Pixel example, not a rule for every phone.

## How to interpret heat on your phone

Use the operating and charging limits published for the exact model and follow its temperature warnings. A warm surface during demanding use does not by itself reveal the cell temperature or prove damage. Repeated warnings, charging pauses, or protective shutdowns are reasons to stop the current workload and use the manufacturer's support path.

This article does not treat removing a case or switching to a slower charger as a universal evidence-based remedy. Accessories, charging systems, and thermal behavior vary by device.
